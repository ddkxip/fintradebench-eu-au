## 4. Clean finite-label vs special-scoring vs non-committed

- **clean finite-label now:** yes_no **37** (36 high-confidence). These map onto the existing framework immediately.
- **numeric needing special scoring: 55** — the gold is a computed/extracted number. A 3-label space (correct/wrong/noncommit) is only meaningful if we score the model's *numeric value* against gold with a tolerance and then derive the label. Treat as `numeric_freeform` + derived label, never as a string-matched category.
- **low-confidence proposals needing rework: 46** (claim_verification 28, comparison_direction 10, company_or_entity_choice 3, open_explanation 5).
- **proposed non-committed golds: 19**, but only **4** of those are genuine; the rest are artifacts of my fallback rules (`flat_or_mixed` default, `schema_uncertain` for long prose).
- **golds containing a genuine decline/none phrase: 4/150 (~3%)**:
  - `financebench_id_00476` — There are none
  - `financebench_id_00723` — Performance is not measured through operating margin
  - `financebench_id_00720` — Performance is not measured through gross margin
  - `financebench_id_00746` — There are none

### ⚠ The single most important scoping finding

FinanceBench golds are **overwhelmingly committed**: only ~4/150 (3%) decline to answer, versus **22/139 (15.8%)** non-committal golds in FinTradeBench. Consequences for the hedge-collision framework:

1. The **'correct non-commitment' cell is nearly empty** — we cannot estimate how often hedging is *right*, which was a key FinTradeBench result (hedging correct ~79% when the gold hedges).
2. `p_noncommit`'s AUROC would be **almost purely mechanical** here (nearly every hedge collides with a committed gold), so the committed-gold-vs-non-committed-gold *contrast* that made the FinTradeBench decomposition informative largely disappears.
3. What FinanceBench **can** test cleanly: the **hedge-collision rate itself**, the **wrong-direction commitment rate**, and the **AU-reduction / EU-flow behaviour** under debate — i.e. three of the four headline findings, on a genuinely independent benchmark.
4. Bonus natural experiment: the yes/no answer space here is `[yes, no, insufficient_data]` with **no soft hedge label** (`mixed`/`conditional` do not exist). That is structurally the 'directional_menu' arm of our hedge-attractor probe — it directly tests the earlier finding that removing soft hedges makes models migrate to `insufficient_data` rather than commit.

## 7. Is FinanceBench-150 suitable for the hedge-collision framework?

**Partially — as a targeted generalization test, not a like-for-like replication.**

Suitable because: (a) 100% oracle-evidence coverage means the 'reasoning conditional on gold evidence' design transfers exactly; (b) it is a genuinely independent benchmark (different companies, documents, annotators, question construction); (c) the yes/no subset (37 items) is immediately schema-compatible; (d) it can test hedge-collision rate, wrong-direction rate, and AU/EU debate dynamics.

Not suitable for: (a) the **non-committed-gold analyses** (correct-noncommitment rate, the AUROC inversion on non-committed golds) — too few non-committal golds; (b) **lane-structured claims** — FinanceBench has no F/T/FT analogue, so the model×lane regime map cannot be replicated; (c) **numeric items** without a new tolerance-based scorer.

Headline caveat to state in any writeup: with only ~3% non-committal golds, a high hedge-collision share on FinanceBench is **partly guaranteed by the benchmark's construction** and must not be reported as an independent confirmation of the FinTradeBench rate without that caveat.
